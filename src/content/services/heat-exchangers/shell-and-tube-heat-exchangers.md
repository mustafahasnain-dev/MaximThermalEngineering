faqs:
  - q: What is a shell and tube heat exchanger used for?
    a: It is used to heat or cool one liquid with another, for example cooling hot oil with water or heating water with steam. It handles higher pressures and temperatures than most other exchanger types, which is why it is common in process plants. [verify]
  - q: Which fluid should go through the tubes?
    a: Usually the dirtier, more corrosive or higher pressure fluid goes in the tubes, because tubes are easier to clean and cheaper to make from special materials than the shell. [verify] We decide this during design from your fluid data.
  - q: Can you retube my existing shell and tube exchanger?
    a: "[verify] Yes, retubing is often possible when the shell and tubesheets are sound. Send the drawing or nameplate and photos of the tubesheet."
  - q: How do I know if a tube is leaking?
    a: Signs are one fluid appearing in the other, such as oil in the cooling water, or a pressure drop on the high pressure side. A pressure test on each side confirms the leak. [verify]
  - q: How often should a shell and tube exchanger be cleaned?
    a: It depends on the fluids. Cooling water sides often need cleaning once a year or more, while clean oil sides need it far less. Rising outlet temperature or pressure drop is the signal to clean. [verify]

## Shell and tube compared with plate heat exchangers

| | Shell and tube | Plate |
|---|---|---|
| Pressure and temperature limits | Higher | Lower [verify] |
| Footprint for the same duty | Larger | Smaller |
| Handling dirty fluids | Better | Can block more easily |
| Cleaning | Tubes can be rodded or bundle pulled | Plates opened and cleaned |

[verify] This comparison is general. We do not list plate heat exchangers as a Maxim product.
