faqs:
  - q: Why does a gas engine radiator have two circuits?
    a: Because most turbocharged gas engines need the charge air cooled to a lower temperature than the jacket water. [verify] The low temperature circuit cools the intercooler so the engine can make full power without knocking; the high temperature circuit cools the engine block.
  - q: Have you built radiators for CAT gas engines?
    a: Yes. We supplied a remote radiator with fan system for a CAT 3516 gas engine for the oil and gas industry.
  - q: My gas engine derates in summer. Can the radiator be the cause?
    a: Yes. If the intercooler circuit runs too hot, the engine controller may reduce power to protect the engine. [verify] A blocked or undersized low temperature section is a common cause, and we check it alongside the jacket water section.
  - q: Can one radiator serve two gas engines?
    a: It can, but it is usually better to give each engine its own radiator so one can be serviced while the other runs. We advise on the layout case by case.

## How gas engine cooling differs from diesel

Gas engines are often run continuously as prime power, so the radiator works at or near full load for most of its life. [verify] Many also have a stricter limit on charge air temperature than a diesel of similar size. That makes the low temperature circuit the part to check first when a gas engine loses power on hot days.

## Information we use to size a gas engine radiator

1. Engine model and continuous rating
2. Heat rejection and flow for the jacket water circuit
3. Heat rejection and flow for the intercooler or aftercooler circuit
4. Maximum allowed coolant outlet temperature for each circuit
5. Site ambient temperature, altitude and the space for the radiator and fans
